# Professor Outreach Email — Prompt Template

Use this prompt in Claude Code (in a workspace containing your CV, research
summary, publications, project outcomes, work/job experience, and generic mail
template in the root folder).

Fill in the bracketed sections with the new professor's info each time, then
paste the whole thing to Claude.

---

I'm applying to work with a professor for a PhD/research position. Use ALL of
the following, already available in this workspace, as source material:
- My CV
- My research summary
- My publications
- My project outcomes/results (not just project titles — the actual findings,
  metrics, or deliverables)
- My work experience / job history
- The generic mail template in the root folder as a structural base

Pull from whichever of these actually contains the strongest, most relevant
match — don't limit yourself to publications alone if a project outcome or
work experience is a better fit for this specific professor.

**Professor info:**
- Google Scholar: [paste link]
- Institutional portfolio / lab page: [paste link]
- Recent publications / lab focus: [paste details or list titles, if already known]

**Research step (do this before drafting):**
Go through the professor's Google Scholar profile and institutional/lab page and
read the ABSTRACTS of as many of their papers as possible, with priority on:
1. The first few papers listed on Google Scholar (usually sorted by year/citations —
   treat these as most representative of current/high-priority work).
2. Any papers, projects, or descriptions featured on their institutional/lab page
   (these are often hand-picked by the professor as their current focus).
3. Papers from the last 1-2 years specifically, even if further down the list.

From these abstracts, build a short internal picture of:
- Recurring themes/methods across their body of work (not just one paper).
- Where their focus seems to be shifting or concentrating recently.
- Any stated open problems, limitations, or future-work directions they mention.

Use this broader picture — not just one paper — to find the strongest, most
specific alignment with my past work/publications, AND to identify directions
I could plausibly contribute to or pursue in the future under their guidance.

**Task:**
Write the BODY of a cold email to this professor (under 120 words, excluding
greeting/signature) in this voice and tone:
- Grounded and specific — built on naming actual past roles/projects and actual
  techniques/methods from the professor's work, not abstract statements.
- Genuinely enthusiastic where it fits naturally (e.g., "I loved...", "I found
  it fascinating...", "quite innovative and interesting to..."), but still
  professional — this is a PhD inquiry, not a casual note.
- No aggressive self-promotion, no overselling — credibility comes from
  specificity, not from claiming to be a great fit.

Structure the body as:

1. Open with 1-2 concrete, specific experiences from my past work/projects —
   named roles, teams, or projects (e.g., "I was the [role] of [named project/team]",
   "I worked with [named group] on [specific technical work]", "Even at [company/
   project], my work is based on..."). Stack 2-3 short, specific examples if
   relevant material exists, to build credibility through specificity rather
   than abstraction.
2. Add a short transition line connecting these experiences to a genuine current
   interest — e.g., "That is why I am interested in...", "This is what has drawn
   me to...".
3. Reference specifically what I found in their work using natural, varied
   connector phrases in the flow of the example below — mix and combine phrases
   like: "I came across your work/research on...", "I loved the work you did
   with...", "I am also going through your work on...", "I was also intrigued by
   your work on...", "I was not aware of/that... before, but it is quite
   innovative/interesting to...". Each connector should be followed by a specific
   named technique, method, or paper topic from their work (not vague references),
   so it reads as genuine, detailed engagement rather than a template. Include
   2-3 such references if the source material supports it.
4. Close by stating plainly that I am seeking to pursue a PhD under their
   supervision, followed by a simple, low-pressure ask (e.g., openings for PhD
   students, a short call, or reviewing my CV).

Weave the phrases naturally into a flowing paragraph rather than listing them
mechanically — the goal is the same organic, one-thought-leads-to-the-next
quality as the reference example below, not a checklist reading.

Prefer concrete outcomes (results, metrics, what was built/solved) and specific
named techniques/methods over vague descriptions or just naming a project or
paper title — specificity is what makes this convincing.

Before matching, first identify from the abstracts and info gathered which 1-2
papers/projects are the professor's MOST RECENT or represent where the group is
currently investing effort (new grants, recent preprints, latest publications,
updated lab page focus, recurring themes across abstracts) — don't default to
their oldest/most-cited paper just because it's most well-known.

**Constraints:**
- No filler phrases ("I am writing to express my interest...").
- No aggressive self-promotion, no calling myself an "asset" or "perfect fit,"
  no overselling my qualifications — credibility comes from specific, named
  experience and specific, named engagement with their work.
- No repeating my whole CV — pick the 2-3 most relevant, specific items only,
  whether from publications, projects, or work experience. Include links/names
  of teams or projects where available (as in the example style).
- Match tone: specific, genuinely engaged, professional, respectful of their time.
- Explicitly state the intent to pursue a PhD under their supervision — don't
  leave this implied.
- Keep it under 120 words total.
- Use the generic mail template's structure/greeting/sign-off, only rewrite the body.

**Reference example (for style/tone only — do not reuse its content):**
"I myself was the Team Lead of my university [Mars Rover Team]. I also had the
opportunity to work with [ANTS], where I was part of a research team working on
multi UAV formation flying. Even at [Company], my work is based on edge camera
networks for event detection and analysis. That is why I am thoroughly interested
in edge computing for robotics and real time data analysis/control.
I came across your research group recently and loved the work you did with
heterogeneous computing units (DRA and GPU) with the knapsack approach to optimize
model inference. I was also intrigued in seeing your empirical work on quantization
methods and TensorRT. I am also going through your work on IDK cascades. I was not
aware of such an idea before skimming the paper but it is quite innovative and
interesting to experiment on."

Before writing, briefly tell me: (a) the recurring themes and current direction
you found across the professor's abstracts/lab page, (b) which of their recent
works/interests you identified as most current, and (c) which of my papers/
projects/outcomes/experiences you're matching to it and why — including, if
relevant, a direction I could pursue in future work with them. Then give me the
final email body.
