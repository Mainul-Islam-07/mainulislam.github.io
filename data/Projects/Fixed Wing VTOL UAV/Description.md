This project involves the development, construction, and outdoor manual testing of a small-scale fixed-wing VTOL UAV prototype featuring a 2-meter rectangular wingspan, brushless motor-driven propulsion, and four aerodynamic control surfaces (two wing ailerons and two V-tail ruddervators). The UAV structure employs polystyrene foam for core bulk, 3D-printed PLA and laser cut PVC sheet for precision parts and skeleton, and polyurethane foam for added resilience, balancing low-weight and manufacturability with sufficient mechanical strength and crash tolerance. The prototype serves as a dedicated empirical platform to analyze aerodynamic stability, control response, and transition dynamics between VTOL and fixed-wing flight regimes, all critical precursors to heavy-payload UAV validation. Aerodynamically, the project emphasizes the hazardous yet essential transition corridor, where careful control allocation and mixing strategies across the wing and V-tail surfaces mitigate the risks of stall, loss of control, or structural overload. Brushless motor sizing and integration is optimized for rapid thrust response and efficient power consumption, with cooling and reliability characterized empirically to support scaling toward higher-power, payload-carrying platforms. Structural design studies focus on low-density, foam-based airframes with PLA and PVC reinforcement, utilizing iterative flight testing and post-flight inspection to validate modeling and optimize joint assemblies and impact resistance. The experimental flight program applies standardized pre-flight checks and manual piloting to instrumented free-flight testing, allowing direct measurement of handling qualities, stability margins, and failure behaviors in a real-world context. Rich data acquisition and telemetry systems—integrating IMUs, GPS, servo feedback, and power sensors—enable high-resolution analysis of aerodynamic coefficients, control effectiveness, and transition behavior, with all results feeding into multi-criteria performance evaluation, model validation, and future controller/autopilot development. The project's outcomes provide critical empirical data and validated methodologies that directly inform scaling for a 50 kg-payload VTOL UAV, establishing confidence in the aerodynamic design, control strategy, and structural philosophy underpinning robust, certifiable heavy-lift UAV platforms. Overall, this prototype serves as a pivotal technological bridge, accelerating the transition from experimental concept to operational, heavy-payload-capable VTOL UAVs in a scalable, cost-effective, and risk-limited manner.
